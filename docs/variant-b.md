# Open Science at TUM

The TUM supports following Open Science principles for making research openly accessible, verifiable and reusable — from
literature and data to software and methods.

## TUM Open Science Strategy

??? info "TUM Open Science Policy"

    The Technical University of Munich (TUM) is committed to Open Science as a foundation of excellent, responsible, and impactful research. Open Science promotes openness, transparency, and reproducibility throughout the research process, thereby strengthening research quality, fostering trust in scientific findings, enabling collaboration and participation, and supporting the efficient dissemination and reuse of knowledge. Open Science extends beyond the publication of research results to include the sharing of methods, protocols, software, research data, and other components of the research process. Its full potential can only be realized when openness is accompanied by high scientific standards and comprehensive documentation from the outset. At the same time, TUM recognizes that openness must be balanced with legal, ethical, security-related, and contractual responsibilities. Decisions on sharing research outputs and underlying materials should therefore be guided by both the goal of transparency and legal, ethical, security-related and contractual obligations.

    Members of TUM are expected to apply the principles of Open Science in their research, with particular attention to the following core aspects.

    **Open Access:** Researchers are encouraged to make their publications openly accessible wherever possible and to follow TUM’s Open Access Policy for concrete guidance.

    **Open Data:** Research data underlying published results should be made public, together with appropriate metadata and in line with the FAIR principles, unless legal, ethical, security-related, or contractual reasons restrict sharing. Suitable disciplinary repositories, generic repositories, or TUM’s mediaTUM may be used.

    **Open Source:** Research software should, where possible, be made publicly available, citable, versioned, and released under a licence that enables reuse. Non-proprietary software and open file formats should be preferred whenever feasible.

    **Open Methodology:** Relevant methods, protocols, and analysis workflows should be documented and, where appropriate, shared to support transparency and reproducibility. In disciplines where it is established, preregistration of research projects should be considered.

    The use of AI offers the opportunity to simplify following Open Science principles by, e.g., by lowering the threshold for adopting workflow management systems, containerised software environments, structured metadata and thorough documentation. At the same time, AI assistance must not undermine transparency, accountability, or reproducibility of scientific output, which must remain verifiable by established scientific means.

    TUM leads by example in advancing Open Science. The university provides training, guidance, and sustainable infrastructure to support transparent and well-documented research practices throughout the research lifecycle, e.g. by offering the documentation tools TUM DataTagger and TUM eLabFTW. It promotes open dissemination through publishing support, funding, the repository mediaTUM, and services that increase the visibility and accessibility of research outputs and activities. TUM invests in robust, sustainable, and predominantly open-source infrastructures, combining in-house developments with community-driven solutions. Furthermore, TUM fosters dialogue between research and society through public engagement and citizen science activities, and strengthens Open Science competencies through educational offerings that are widely accessible, including to external audiences.
